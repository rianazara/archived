The structural idea: returning residents pass through a transitional status
(**RNOR** — resident but not ordinarily resident) before becoming fully
resident (**ROR**), and the two are taxed very differently.

- **During RNOR**, foreign income kept abroad is generally not taxed in
  India, which creates a planning window for exits, reallocations and
  restructuring.
- **Once ROR**, global income becomes taxable in India, foreign assets must
  be disclosed, and every sale is a reportable event in both countries
  (treaty relief exists but reporting doesn't go away).

What the post suggests doing with the window:

- **Don't liquidate everything on the way out** — immediate blanket selling
  can trigger US capital gains and poorly-timed Indian exposure. Stagger
  across RNOR years instead.
- **Retirement accounts**: leaving them in place preserves tax-deferred
  growth, rolling into an IRA gives more control, and withdrawing is usually
  worst because of penalties plus tax. If you don't need the cash, don't
  touch it.
- **Equities**: timing matters more than selection here, because rebalancing
  and gain harvesting are cheaper inside the window than outside it.
- **Roth accounts are the awkward case** — tax-free in the US, but Indian
  treatment is genuinely unsettled.

**Treat this as vocabulary, not a plan.** Residency thresholds, treaty
positions and the treatment of specific account types turn on details this
post can't address, they change with budgets, and errors here are expensive
and hard to unwind. The actual move is a cross-border tax professional
before the move, not after.
