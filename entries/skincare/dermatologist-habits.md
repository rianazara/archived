From someone describing ten years of practice, with the framing that it
isn't purely genetics:

- **Sunscreen always**, and *tinted* if you're prone to dark spots — the
  tint adds protection against visible light, which plain SPF doesn't cover.
- **Never sleep in makeup.** Micellar water or a cleansing balm removes it
  far better than wipes and with less irritation; follow with a gentle
  cleanser.
- **Don't pop pimples** — it's what converts a spot into a mark or scar.
  Hydrocolloid patches cover it, stop the picking, and speed healing.
- **Barrier creams** (the cica/panthenol type) for flaky, irritated skin,
  rather than pushing through with actives.
- **Exfoliation** brightens quickly, and an inexpensive peel can do a lot of
  what costlier procedures promise.
- **Tretinoin plus azelaic acid** is the combination that's both popular and
  dermatologist-endorsed: collagen, fine lines and acne at once.
- **Dermaplaning** for smoother makeup application — on clean skin, with a
  gentle cleanser or hyaluronic serum for slip, in the direction of hair
  growth, slowly.

Two notes the post doesn't make clearly. Tretinoin is **prescription** in
most places and **not safe in pregnancy or while nursing**. And "always
sunscreen plus three actives" is a lot to start at once — introducing one
at a time is how you find out which one your skin objects to.
