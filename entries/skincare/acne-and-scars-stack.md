Framed as prevention and consistency rather than treatment — find products
you tolerate and stay on them.

| Step | Active | Doing what |
|---|---|---|
| Cleanser | benzoyl peroxide | kills acne bacteria |
| Treatment | azelaic acid (15% prescription) | anti-inflammatory, evens tone |
| Retinoid | tretinoin or adapalene | reduces oil, improves marks |
| Brightening serum | kojic acid, tranexamic acid, arbutin, vitamin C | fades hyperpigmented marks |
| Daytime | tinted mineral SPF | prevents the marks darkening again |

The logic worth extracting: acne and *acne marks* are two different
problems treated simultaneously — the retinoid and benzoyl peroxide address
the acne, the brightening actives address the pigment left behind, and
sunscreen is what stops you undoing the second group daily.

Practical cautions the post skips. Benzoyl peroxide bleaches fabric and is
drying; retinoids and acids stacked together commonly cause irritation, so
alternate nights rather than layering at first; adapalene is available over
the counter in many places while tretinoin is prescription and unsafe in
pregnancy. "Lightweight products" matters because an occlusive moisturizer
undermines the rest.

_General information, not medical advice — a dermatologist is the right
route for prescription actives._
