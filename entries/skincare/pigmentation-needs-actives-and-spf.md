The central point: for melasma and stubborn dark spots the question isn't
whether a brand is good, it's whether the formula contains something that
acts on a pigment pathway.

- **Mechanism matters.** Thiamidol, for instance, is used in
  pigment-focused products because it inhibits **tyrosinase**, an enzyme in
  melanin production. "Brightening" with no named active and no mechanism
  is a marketing claim.
- **Price is close to irrelevant.** What decides the outcome is the active,
  the formulation, consistency of use, and whether your skin tolerates it —
  not cost or luxury feel.
- **Without daily sunscreen, none of it holds.** Pigmentation re-triggers
  easily on UV *and visible light* exposure, which is the argument for
  tinted or iron-oxide-containing sunscreen specifically.

So the practical order is: dermatologist-guided pigment care plus rigorous
sun protection, rather than rotating through brightening products.

Worth knowing melasma is notably stubborn and recurrence-prone — it's
managed rather than cured, and hormonal triggers (pregnancy, contraceptives)
and heat matter alongside sun. The post gated its full conversation behind a
comment, and advertises a specific ingredient; treat the mechanism as the
takeaway rather than the product.
