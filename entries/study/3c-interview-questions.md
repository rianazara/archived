You can rehearse answers for days and still improvise your questions, and
that part gets scored too. The cited research finding: people who asked more
questions — follow-ups especially — were liked more.

Three types, with when to deploy each:

- **Clarify** (one or two, during the interview) — when a question is vague,
  ask for context before answering. Proves you're listening rather than
  pattern-matching to a rehearsed answer.
- **Connect** (one or two, near the end) — get the interviewer talking about
  their own path and why they've stayed. People enjoy being asked, and the
  good feeling attaches to you.
- **Collaborate** (one, as your closer) — ask about a real problem the team
  is working on right now. As they describe it, they start picturing you
  solving it.

The sequencing is the clever part: clarify establishes competence mid-flow,
connect builds warmth, collaborate ends on you inside the role.

Complements [[questions-to-ask-at-the-end]], which is a longer menu — this
is the structure for choosing from it. Treat the "interviewers weigh
psychology over technical answers" claim as overstated; rapport decides
between comparable candidates rather than replacing competence.
