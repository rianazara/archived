The point made about AI product interviews: trust, safety and risk can't be
appended after you've described the solution. They belong in how you frame
the problem and design the feature in the first place.

Why the difference is visible to an interviewer — "and we'd add guardrails"
tacked on at the end reads as a checklist item. The same concern raised
while defining the problem ("the failure mode here is confidently wrong
output on a medical query, so the bar for a human-in-the-loop step is in
the requirements") shows you understand that AI products fail differently
from other software.

Concretely, that means naming during problem definition: what unacceptable
failure looks like, which users bear the cost of an error, where a human
must stay in the loop, and what you'd measure to know it's happening.

This is the same instinct as the evaluation mindset in
[[ai-pm-is-not-a-research-role]] — turning "make it helpful" into
measurable criteria *before* building — and "permissions before autonomy"
from [[seven-layers-of-ai-engineering]].
