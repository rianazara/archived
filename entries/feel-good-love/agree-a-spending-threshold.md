The practice: pick an amount — the example given was $100 — above which a
purchase affecting the household gets talked about before it happens.

The post is explicit that the number isn't the point. What the rule actually
produces is transparency and the absence of financial surprises, and the
right threshold depends entirely on income, priorities and budget. Too low
and it becomes surveillance over groceries; too high and it stops catching
anything.

Why a stated number works better than an understanding: it removes the
judgment call about whether *this* purchase was big enough to mention, which
is where resentment accumulates on both sides — one person feeling
monitored, the other feeling blindsided.

The healthiest version named is agreeing the amount together, which also
makes it revisable as circumstances change rather than a rule one person
imposed.
