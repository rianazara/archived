**Bambi (1942).** Disney brought live deer into the studio so animators
could study how they actually moved — the walk, the ear twitches — which is
why the animation read as unprecedentedly real at the time. The film took
close to five years, with artists studying forests in Vermont and Maine to
get backgrounds that looked painted rather than drawn, producing the soft
watercolour style it's remembered for. It wasn't a commercial success on
release, because the war had gutted theatre audiences worldwide; its
reputation accumulated afterwards.

**Snow Bear (2025).** A hand-drawn short by Aaron Blaise, made over three
or more years with upwards of 11,000 traditional drawings, built around a
polar bear and carrying a message about warming and declining populations.

What links them is the cost structure of the medium: observation and
repetition, measured in years and thousands of drawings, with no way to
shortcut either. Worth holding next to how cheap generated animation has
become — the Bambi anecdote is interesting precisely because studying real
deer for months was the only available route to that result.
