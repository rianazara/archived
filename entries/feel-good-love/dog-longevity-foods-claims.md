A post crediting five foods — raw honey, frozen blueberries, boiled sweet
potato, parsley and collagen — with the longevity of dogs cared for over
three decades, each with a confident mechanism attached: honey "calming low
grade inflammation that steals years," blueberry anthocyanins protecting
brain and eyesight, sweet potato feeding the gut, parsley supporting kidneys
and liver, collagen for joints.

**Recording this with the caveats, because the claims are the weak part.**

- No sources, no study, no credential stated beyond experience, and the
  detailed "biological mechanisms" sit behind a comment-for-DM gate — the
  structure of a lead magnet rather than of evidence.
- **Honey is sugar.** A teaspoon twice weekly is unlikely to harm a healthy
  adult dog, but it's calories and not advisable for diabetic,
  immunocompromised or very young animals.
- **Parsley in quantity is a genuine caution** — some varieties are not
  advised for dogs, and "supports the kidneys" is exactly the sort of claim
  that matters if an animal already has kidney disease.
- Blueberries and plain cooked sweet potato are uncontroversial as
  occasional treats. Oral collagen's benefits are contested in humans and
  less studied in dogs.
- Survivorship: dogs that lived long were fed these; that isn't evidence the
  foods caused it.

The honest version: a few of these are fine treats, none is established as
a longevity intervention, and anything beyond a treat-sized amount — or any
animal with an existing condition — is a vet question.
