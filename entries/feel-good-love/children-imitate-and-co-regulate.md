The old line it opens with — children may not always listen to their
parents but have never failed to imitate them — and the specifics of what
gets imitated: how you handle stress, how you treat people, how you respond
to frustration, how you communicate, how you care for yourself.

**The part with real grounding:** co-regulation. Young children's nervous
systems genuinely calibrate against the regulated adults around them, which
is why a calm adult presence does more for a dysregulated child than
instructions do. It's also why the parent's own state is a legitimate
intervention point rather than a self-indulgent one.

**The part that's overstated:** "your child's sleep patterns mirror your
stress levels" as a flat claim. Child sleep is driven by a tangle of age,
temperament, routine, environment and health; parental stress is one input
among many, and framing it as a mirror hands parents guilt for something
largely outside their control.

The conclusion is still right and usefully low-pressure: this isn't about
being a perfect parent, it's about noticing that the modelling happens
whether or not you intend it.

Note the source is an institute promoting its own professional training, so
the "parenting advice misses this" framing is positioning.
