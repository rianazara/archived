Karma yoga, compressed: you control your actions, not the results. The
moment you attach to the result, fear arrives — of failure, of judgment, of
losing.

What follows from acting without attachment to outcome:

- Peace stops depending on success.
- Confidence stops depending on approval.
- Effort stops depending on certainty.

Stated as the instruction: do the work, give your best, leave the rest.

Why it's more than consolation — it's a redirection of attention toward the
part that's actually actionable. Most anxiety about work is spent on the
outcome, which is where effort has no purchase, and taken from the execution,
which is where it does.

Worth noting what it isn't: indifference to results, or an argument against
planning. The claim is about where to place your sense of self-worth, not
about ignoring whether something worked — and in a professional context you
still measure outcomes, you just don't let them adjudicate your value.
