Reflexively answering "yes, of course" gives up two things: warning for your
referee, and information about where you actually stand.

The stronger reply agrees in principle and attaches a question — happy to
provide references at the appropriate stage, when are reference checks
happening? That reads as confident rather than evasive, buys time to brief
the person, and tells you how far along the process is.

Also worth remembering: a reference doesn't have to be your current manager.
A previous manager, a project lead, a senior colleague, or a client you
worked closely with can all serve — the test is who can speak concretely
about your work and impact, not who sits above you on the current org chart.
