Three variants of the same squeeze, and the same underlying move: decline
the document or the history, then redirect to range, scope, or market value
while keeping the conversation open.

**"What's your salary history?"** — In many US states employers may not ask;
know the local rule. Otherwise: share a target range instead, or redirect to
what's competitive for the role's scope rather than what you were paid
before.

**"Can you show proof of your other offer?"** — They're usually gauging your
timeline, comparing packages, or seeking internal approval to move. You
don't have to send the letter, which contains another company's confidential
terms. Acknowledge the offer exists, decline to share the document, and
offer what actually helps: the shape of the package and your decision date.
A flat "that's confidential" protects you but shuts the conversation down.

**"You already earn more — can you join at the same CTC?"** — Open with
genuine alignment on the role, then state you're looking for a move that
reflects compensation growth, justify it on experience and market rather
than need, and ask for the budgeted range so the discussion runs on data.
Widen it beyond base: bonus, joining bonus, growth path.

One hard rule: never invent a competing offer to manufacture urgency. A
follow-up question you can't answer costs you the trust the whole
negotiation rests on.
