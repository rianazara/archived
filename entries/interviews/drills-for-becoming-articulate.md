Practice habits, as distinct from in-the-moment technique:

1. **60-second explanation** — explain something you know well, out loud, no
   notes. Exposes exactly where your thinking gets muddy.
2. **Replace one vague word a day** — swap "good," "nice," "interesting" for
   the word that actually fits. More precise, not fancier.
3. **One-sentence summaries** of a meeting, book, or podcast. Trains you to
   find the main point fast, which is what stops rambling.
4. **Finish the sentence instead of restarting it.** Don't abandon a thought
   mid-way for a better phrasing; finish, then polish.
5. **Keep a phrase bank** — when someone explains something well, save the
   structure of the thought, not just the quote.
6. **Answer random questions aloud** with 30 seconds and no prep, so you
   practice retrieving thoughts rather than reciting rehearsed ones.
7. **Practice transitions** — "the reason I mention this is…", "what changed
   my mind was…", "the bigger point is…".
8. **Read difficult writing aloud** to absorb rhythm, sentence shape, and
   where emphasis falls.
9. **Explain one idea three ways** — to a friend, to a ten-year-old, to
   someone who disagrees. Knowing isn't the same as conveying.
10. **Pause instead of filling silence.** Two seconds of quiet is not an
    emergency, and reads better than "um" or three unnecessary sentences.
