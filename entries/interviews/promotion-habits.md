From someone describing five promotions in ten years in tech:

1. **Keep a running list of wins** — weekly: what you accomplished, the
   business impact, positive feedback, problems solved, work beyond your
   scope. By review season the evidence already exists.
2. **Make sure your manager knows what you're working on.** Don't assume
   visibility; use 1:1s for focus, wins, results, and where you need
   support.
3. **Ask about the next level before promotion season** — specifically, what
   you'd need to demonstrate for them to say you're already operating at
   that level, then pin down expected scope, behaviors and outcomes.
4. **Speak up in meetings with senior leaders present**, without needing the
   perfect answer — a question or a point of view counts.
5. **Start doing work that resembles the next job** — cross-functional
   leadership, ambiguous problems, higher-stakes decisions, mentoring.

The organizing idea: aim for the promotion to read as confirmation of what
you're already doing, rather than a request.
