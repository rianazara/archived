"I'm good, thanks" isn't wrong, it's forgettable — and it puts both people on
autopilot for the opening moments, which is when the tone gets set.

Better is a real answer that gives the other person something to react to:
naming that you're a little nervous but genuinely interested because the
role matches what you want to do, or mentioning what you were just working
on and that the timing feels good.

The mechanism is simple enough — it humanizes you, wakes the interviewer up,
and starts a conversation rather than a questionnaire.

Keep it honest, though. The reason this works is that it's a genuine answer;
a rehearsed line about being nervous-but-excited delivered flatly is just a
different script. If you aren't nervous, say something true instead.
