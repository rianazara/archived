The framing: at lower levels you compete on skills; higher up everyone in
the room already has the résumé and the right answers, so purely answering
questions makes you interchangeable.

The shift is to treat the interview as a two-way sales process — qualifying
the company with your own questions rather than only responding. Two example
moves:

- **The in-demand frame** — noting that most companies inviting you in raise
  the same problem, then asking whether they see it too and what they think
  causes it. Signals you're sought after and that you understand the
  landscape.
- **The ROI frame** — instead of asking about onboarding like a student, ask
  what onboarding would look like so you can understand operations and
  deliver results quickly. Positions you as a solution rather than a hire.

Treat with some caution: this is coaching-industry framing and the
"status over skills" claim is asserted, not evidenced. The transferable part
is sound — asking informed questions and not performing desperation — but
the scripts can read as manufactured if delivered without real substance
behind them.
