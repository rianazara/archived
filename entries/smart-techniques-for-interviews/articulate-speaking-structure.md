Observed from someone who came across as unusually composed under
questioning — the claim being that this is structure, not charisma, and
therefore learnable:

- **Answer in one sentence, then expand.** Conclusion first, reasoning
  after, so the listener always knows where they are.
- **Pause before answering, never mid-answer.** A beat before you start
  reads as thinking; a stall halfway through reads as losing the thread.
- **Use numbers instead of "a lot."** "Eleven markets," "two years" —
  specificity signals you've been paying attention.
- **Say "I don't know" without changing tone or pace**, then give a date for
  following up. Done flatly, it makes everything else more credible.
- **Don't repeat the question back.** It's a time-buying tic that costs the
  first seconds of every answer.
- **One comparison per point.** Over-explaining and being clear are
  frequently opposites.
- **Stop when you're done.** No trailing sentence, no scanning for approval.

The underlying idea: people who sound spontaneous have usually decided in
advance how they build a sentence.
