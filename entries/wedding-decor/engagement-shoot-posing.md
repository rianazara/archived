The argument: a good engagement shoot isn't about finding a perfect
location, it's about knowing how to be with each other in front of a camera.

The pose types named — soft forehead kisses, candid walking shots, hand-held
portraits, cinematic over-the-shoulder looks — all share a structure worth
noticing: they give people **something to do** rather than something to
hold. Walking, touching, looking at each other instead of the lens. Posed
stillness facing the camera is what produces stiff photographs.

The practical instruction is the useful bit: pick the few you'd actually
want and **send them to your photographer in advance**, so the shoot isn't
spent inventing direction on the spot.

The reason "location doesn't matter much" holds up: a tight frame of two
people reacting to each other works almost anywhere, while a spectacular
backdrop can't rescue two people standing awkwardly in front of it.
