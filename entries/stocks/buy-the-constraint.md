The argument: the first wave of a trend is owning the obvious leaders. The
next question is where the bottleneck is forming, because that's where
pricing power concentrates.

Illustrated with three thematic funds at different widths:

- **Broad semiconductor exposure** — large designers and equipment makers
  across the stack. The default if you don't want to pick a constraint.
- **The memory bottleneck** — concentrated in HBM/DRAM/NAND names. One
  saved breakdown had its top three holdings at roughly 28%, 27% and 20%,
  which is the whole thesis in three positions.
- **The lithography and photonics layer** — equipment and optical
  interconnect. Narrower, smaller fund, targeting precision manufacturing.

Other saved posts extend the same logic into nuclear and grid power,
robotics, and space.

**The caveat the framing invites you to skip:** "buy the constraint" and
"concentrate heavily" are the same sentence. A fund whose top three holdings
are three-quarters of the portfolio is a sector bet wearing a
diversification costume, and if the bottleneck resolves — new capacity,
substitution, demand shifting — the thesis and the position unwind together.
Narrow thematic funds also tend to be newer, smaller and less liquid, which
is exactly the setup the NAV warning in
[[etfs-versus-stocks-two-checks]] describes.
