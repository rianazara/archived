The tradeoff, stated without a winner: individual stocks give more control
and more upside if you want to do company research; funds give thematic
exposure without tracking twenty tickers or opening accounts to access
foreign markets.

The useful part is that **a fund is not set-and-forget**, and two things are
worth checking on any of them:

**1. Expense ratio.** The annual charge for holding it. The difference
between 0.75% and 0.20% looks trivial and compounds into a real drag over
decades. Cheaper isn't automatically better — but you should know what
you're paying and what it buys.

**2. NAV versus market price.** NAV is what the underlying holdings are
worth; market price is what it trades at. A wide gap means you're paying a
premium or buying at a discount to the real value. This matters most in
thinly traded, niche funds — which is precisely the kind the
bottleneck-chasing in [[buy-the-constraint]] points you toward.

Worth adding a third: **what's actually inside it.** Thematic funds are
named for a story, and the holdings sometimes only loosely match it.
