Full courses published free by universities, roughly from theory to applied:

- **MIT, Finance Theory I** (OpenCourseWare) — asset pricing, arbitrage,
  market equilibrium, with lecture notes and problem sets.
- **Yale, Financial Markets** (Robert Shiller, Coursera) — how stocks,
  bonds and derivatives function, plus behavioural finance, bubbles and
  crises with historical cases.
- **Wharton / UPenn, Introduction to Corporate Finance** — time value of
  money, risk-return, valuation basics. One of the most-taken finance MOOCs.
- **Columbia, Corporate Finance** — valuation, capital structure,
  discounted cash flow, leverage, MBA-style cases.
- **Harvard CS50 Finance** — financial modelling and portfolio construction
  in Python, including building a trading simulator against live data.
- **Princeton, Bitcoin & Cryptocurrency Technologies** — blockchain, mining
  and cryptographic security, with programming assignments.
- **Stanford, machine learning applied to trading** — regression,
  reinforcement learning, backtesting.

Why this is the most durable entry in this collection: the Shiller and MIT
material is about how markets behave, which stays true, while most of the
other saved posts are snapshots of a particular quarter's trade.

Start with Yale if the goal is understanding markets, Wharton if the goal is
valuing a company.
