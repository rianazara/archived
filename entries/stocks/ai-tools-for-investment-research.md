Two saved posts describe the same workflow: pointing an assistant at
financial material so it can summarize earnings calls, pull out what changed
from a transcript, build a valuation model in a spreadsheet, structure
backtests, and organize strategy comparisons.

What this genuinely helps with — reading volume. Earnings transcripts,
filings and reports are long, repetitive and structured, which is exactly
where summarization and extraction work well. Turning three hours of reading
into a reviewable set of notes is a real gain.

What it doesn't do, and both posts are more honest about this than most:

- **It doesn't remove risk.** Organizing research into a data-driven process
  changes your confidence, not the distribution of outcomes.
- **A backtest is not a return.** It's a description of the past fitted on
  data you chose, and the more strategies you compare the more likely the
  best one is overfitted.
- **Extracted numbers need checking against the filing.** Summarization
  errors in financial figures are quiet and consequential.

One of the two posts is a gated marketplace-link funnel, and its claim that
"the gap between retail investors and hedge funds is shrinking fast" is the
part I'd discount hardest — the gap is capital, access, execution and data,
none of which a plugin changes.

Reasonable use: a drafting and reading aid whose output you verify. See
[[ask-for-reasoning-and-uncertainty]] for making the output auditable.

_Educational summary, not investment advice; I'm not a licensed advisor._
