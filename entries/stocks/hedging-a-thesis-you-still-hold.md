The idea worth extracting, separate from whose trades it was: a fund stayed
long the AI infrastructure thesis — keeping infrastructure exposure, adding
data-centre and mining names — while simultaneously opening large put
positions against the most crowded semiconductor names.

That's not a contradiction, and the distinction is the lesson. "This
buildout is real" and "these specific stocks are priced as though they
cannot lose" are separate claims. You can hold the first and hedge the
second, because consensus positioning is its own risk independent of whether
the underlying trend plays out.

What makes it instructive is the asymmetry it protects against: if the
thesis is right but the crowded trade de-rates, an unhedged position gives
back the gains anyway.

**Three cautions on this material.** Disclosed holdings are a snapshot and
stale by the time they're public, so they describe what someone held, not
what they hold. Hedging with options carries risks — premium decay,
leverage, timing — that none of these posts mention. And one saved post on
this fund was an **advertisement for a copy-trading service** that
automatically mirrors the picks through your brokerage, with "every single
pick has moved up" as the hook. Recent performance as a sales pitch is the
oldest one there is.
