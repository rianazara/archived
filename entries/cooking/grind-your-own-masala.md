Two distinct fresh-ground patterns appear in the saved recipes, and they
aren't interchangeable.

**Dry-roast and grind** (for a dry North Indian sabzi like aloo gobi matar):
dry roast ~2 tbsp coriander seeds, 1 tsp cumin, 1 tsp black peppercorns and
4–5 dried red chillies, then grind fine. Added late in cooking, 3–4 tbsp for
a kadai of vegetables, alongside Kashmiri chilli powder for colour.

**Roast in fat and grind wet** (for a coconut-based kootu): sauté urad dal,
black peppercorns, dried and green chillies, curry leaves and grated coconut
in coconut oil, add cumin off the heat so it doesn't burn, then grind to a
paste with a little water. Stirred in after the dals and vegetables are
pressure cooked, then simmered briefly.

Two details worth carrying over:

- **Cumin goes in off the heat** in the wet version — residual heat is
  enough, and it turns bitter quickly.
- The dry version uses **more cumin than mustard** in the tempering, which
  is the opposite of the default proportion and noticeably changes the
  aroma.

Generous asafoetida is treated as structural rather than optional in the
aloo gobi — added both with the potatoes and in the seasoning.
