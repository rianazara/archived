A post offering LLM portfolio projects "with resume bullets that get past
ATS." The project ideas are reasonable and each is a weekend or two of work:

- **Resume screener** — score and rank resumes against a job description.
- **Structured extraction** — pull names, dates and amounts out of invoices
  or contracts.
- **Multi-turn agent** — a chatbot that holds context across a whole
  conversation rather than the last message.

**The hazard is the bullets.** They come pre-filled with precise figures —
agreement rates with human reviewers, field-level accuracy, percentage
reductions in review time, context-retention scores. Those numbers describe
a project you haven't run. Copying them onto a resume is fabricating
evidence, and it collapses the moment an interviewer asks how the number was
measured, what the baseline was, or how large the evaluation set was.

Use the structure, supply your own numbers: build the thing, measure it
honestly, and report what you actually got — including when it's less
impressive than the template. A modest real figure you can defend for ten
minutes beats an excellent invented one you can't defend for thirty seconds.

This is the same discipline as [[proving-an-agent-works]]: the eval harness
is what lets you make a quantitative claim and stand behind it.
