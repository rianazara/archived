The argument: shipping stopped being the hard part, and being understood
became it. Teams release features weekly that customers scroll past, and
"powered by AI" conveys nothing about what the product does for anyone.

The consequence stated bluntly — products with worse technology routinely
win on a clearer story.

Why this follows from everything else getting cheap: when building is
expensive, the artifact is the differentiator. When building is cheap,
distribution and comprehension are, because a user's decision to care is now
the scarce resource rather than an engineer's time.

The practical test for any feature description: replace "AI" with what
actually happens for the user and see whether the sentence still says
something. "Finds the clause in your contract that changed" survives;
"AI-powered document intelligence" doesn't.

Connects to [[prd-before-you-build]] — if you can't say who the user is and
what problem is being solved, the marketing copy was never going to rescue
it. The post is promoting a webinar on a "Product Storyteller" framework;
the diagnosis is the part worth keeping.
