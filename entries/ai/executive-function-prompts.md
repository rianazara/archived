**First, a correction.** The post announces this as "a feature called ADHD
Executive Function Mode." There is no such feature, in Claude or anywhere
else — these are ordinary prompts. Nothing is being unlocked, and the claim
of finishing a week's work in four hours is engagement framing. The prompts
themselves are still useful, which is why this entry exists.

Seven patterns, paraphrased:

- **Break the paralysis** — ask for steps so small each takes under a
  minute, then ask only for the first one and precisely where to start.
- **Build a stimulation menu** — short movement breaks, longer deep-work
  blocks, and brief creative play, organized as options to pick from.
- **Simulate a body double** — have the assistant check in on a fixed
  interval and ask for a status update while you work.
- **Clean the palate between contexts** — ask for a three-minute transition
  routine when switching between tasks of different energy.
- **Attach a boring task to an existing interest** — reframe admin work as a
  quest with a reward at completion.
- **Audit time blindness** — give your estimate, then ask which hidden
  sub-tasks you habitually forget, and re-estimate.
- **Externalize open loops** — dump everything on your mind, have it sorted
  into now / later / discard, and get one next action for the "now" items.

The common mechanism is externalizing a decision you're too depleted to
make: sequencing, estimating, or sorting. That's a real thing to delegate.
Earlier slides of this same material circulated with the prompts withheld
behind a comment gate; this version spelled them out.
