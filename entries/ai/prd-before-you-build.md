The named failure: founders, PMs and people building with AI assistants
start building before they're clear on what they're building.

Questions to answer first:

- Who is the user?
- What problem are they trying to solve?
- What does the ideal user journey look like?
- How will we measure success?
- What could go wrong?
- **What are we intentionally not building?**

The last one does the most work and gets skipped most often — scope stated
only in the positive expands indefinitely, which is especially easy when
generating code is cheap.

The claim behind it: most failed products aren't engineering failures,
they're clarity failures. And the observation that the strongest PMs aren't
the smartest people in the room, they're the clearest thinkers — which
echoes the same line in
[[five-part-prompt-and-negative-constraints]], where specifying what *not*
to do is the underused lever. Same idea in two places: constraints are where
clarity lives.
