Framed around a free Anthropic workshop on using Claude well. Two things
carried over.

**The five components.** A complete prompt specifies role, context, task,
format and constraints. Omit one and the model fills the gap with a guess,
and its guesses aren't your preferences.

**Negative constraints are underused.** Telling the model what *not* to do
is often faster than specifying what to do — no filler phrases, no passive
voice, no corporate jargon, no unnecessary caveats. Stacking several of
these does more for tone than any amount of positive description.

The line worth keeping: the best prompt engineers aren't the best typists,
they're the clearest thinkers. Each technique is really a thinking technique
wearing a formatting costume — the prompt is the artifact, the thinking is
the skill.

Discount the post's framing ("the model is not the problem, you are" and the
promise of permanent improvement) as engagement styling. The five components
and negative constraints are the substance.
