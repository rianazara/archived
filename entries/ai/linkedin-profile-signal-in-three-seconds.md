The premise is sound: profiles get scanned, not read. If yours doesn't
signal what you are almost immediately, skill doesn't get a chance to
matter. The fix is positioning, keywords and impact framing rather than a
redesign.

**Worth doing:**

- **Headline formula** — `[Role] | [Skills] | [Value]`, so the scan
  resolves instantly.
- **Banner** — name, target role, two or three key skills, contact. Most
  people leave it blank.
- **"About" that sounds human** — a hook, the actual story, one personal
  detail, then how to reach you.
- **Bullets as accomplishment + measure + method** — "accomplished X, as
  measured by Y, by doing Z." Results, not duties.
- **Visual proof** — attach decks, links, writing to the relevant roles.
- **Skills ordered by relevance** to the job you want, with specific
  endorsements requested from people who can speak to them.
- **Job alerts** set frequently, since being early genuinely matters.
- **Privacy/job-seeking settings** reviewed, including recruiter-only
  visibility if you don't want the public banner.

**Treat skeptically:** toggling "Open to Work" off and on to "trigger the
algorithm," and re-uploading your resume to look active. These are folklore
about ranking systems nobody outside the company can see.

**Don't do:** setting your profile location to a city you don't live in.
It's presented as a filter hack, but it's a false statement that surfaces
immediately in a first call, wastes both sides' time on roles you may not be
able to take, and reads as deception at the exact moment you need trust. If
you're genuinely willing to relocate, say so explicitly instead — that's the
honest version of the same signal.
