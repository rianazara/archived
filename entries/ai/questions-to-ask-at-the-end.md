Premise: candidates get rejected less for weak answers than for weak
questions, and the close is where a lukewarm impression can still move.

The strongest of the thirty offered, grouped by what they're actually doing:

**Calibrating the bar**
- What would make someone a no-brainer hire for this?
- What separates good from exceptional here?
- What do top performers do differently?
- Why do people struggle in this role?

**Making the job concrete**
- What problem will this role solve first?
- What does success look like in 90 days?
- What should I prioritize if I start tomorrow?
- What gap is this role filling?

**Reading the team honestly**
- What's not obvious about the culture?
- How does the team handle conflict?
- How do you give feedback?
- What keeps top performers here?

**Closing**
- How do I compare to other candidates?
- Do you have any hesitation about my profile?
- What would make me a clear hire?

That last group is uncomfortable and the most valuable — asking about
hesitation is the only way to address an objection before it's decided
without you in the room.

The instruction attached matters: don't ask all of them. Pick the few that
make the interviewer picture you in the role.
