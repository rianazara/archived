A technique attributed to Felix Rieseberg at Anthropic, aimed at getting the
model to go *deeper* rather than simply produce more.

Before asking for the work, ask the model to think through how a leading
expert would approach the task — what methods and tools they'd reach for,
and how the finished result would be judged. Then have it use that reasoning
to rewrite your original prompt into a more ambitious version, and run that.

The three dimensions it pushes on:

- **Altitude** — operating at a more expert level.
- **Depth** — going further into the problem instead of broader.
- **Range** — surfacing approaches you wouldn't have specified.

Why it works: most prompts encode the asker's own ceiling. Asking what
excellence would look like first, then rewriting, lets the model propose a
better target than you knew to request. The evaluation question is the
quiet lever — defining "good" changes what gets produced.

Two later posts carried the prompt itself, which asks for: what a top
professional would do that you aren't asking for; the *rawest* material
they'd start from rather than something already digested for you; the
specific methods and tools they'd use; and what — other than you or the
model — would show the result is right. Then it rewrites your prompt to ask
for exactly that.

The "rawest material" clause is the sharpest part and the easiest to miss:
it pushes toward primary sources over summaries. And "other than you or me"
forces an *external* check, which is what stops the model grading its own
homework.
