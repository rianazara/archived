The advice is to stop memorizing twenty-five generic situations and prepare
a small set that each prove you already operate a level up:

1. **Your biggest business impact** — what problem, why it was worth
   solving, what changed because of you, and the quantified result.
2. **Influencing a major decision without authority** — who disagreed, what
   they actually cared about, how you brought them along.
3. **A high-stakes call made on incomplete information** — senior roles
   rarely get clean data, so this is where judgment under ambiguity shows.
4. **Something you got wrong** — what you misjudged, what you learned, and
   how it changed how you lead. Needs a real failure, not a near-miss.
5. **A problem you identified before it was assigned** — you saw it, created
   clarity, aligned people, drove the outcome.

The shared arc: **problem → stakes → judgment → influence → impact.**

Note the overlap with [[story-not-star]] — both replace STAR with a
structure that front-loads stakes and ends on learning, because the
interesting part of a senior story is the decision, not the task list.

The post is marketing a paid framework and a masterclass; the five story
types stand on their own.
