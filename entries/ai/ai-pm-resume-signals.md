Six gaps that weaken an AI PM application:

1. **No substantial AI project.** It needn't be a startup — a shipped site,
   tool, agent or workflow solving a real problem. Ideally it shows 0→1
   ownership, prototyping, evals, observability, iteration, and measurable
   impact.
2. **No visible ownership.** "Supported" says nothing. Name what you drove:
   discovery, requirements, prototype, alignment, launch, measurement.
3. **Not showing AI you already use at work.** Your title needn't say AI —
   if you automated a workflow, analyzed data or prototyped faster with an
   assistant, APIs or agents, put it down and quantify it.
4. **No customer or stakeholder evidence.** The job is translating between
   users, engineering and the business: interviews, feedback turned into
   requirements, cross-functional alignment, deciding under ambiguity.
5. **Terminology misused in either direction.** Say RAG, agents, evals, tool
   calling, LLM-as-judge or tracing if you genuinely did them — but cap it
   at one or two AI keywords per bullet. Over-jargoning reads as badly as
   vagueness.
6. **Non-AI experience framed as tasks.** Rewrite around ownership and
   impact: "worked on onboarding" becomes owning the redesign from discovery
   to launch, aligning three functions, and moving completion by a stated
   percentage.

Directly relevant to your resume-tailoring workflow — items 2 and 6 are the
same rewrite rule, and item 5 is a real constraint to encode.
