Five patterns that turn an assistant from an answer machine into a tutor.
The distinction drawn: search gives you answers, this gives you
understanding, and they aren't the same thing.

- **Step teacher** — don't give the answer; break the topic into the
  smallest possible steps, and after each one ask whether it landed before
  continuing. If not, explain differently. Target: you could teach it.
- **Socratic mode** — don't explain at all; ask questions that lead me to it.
  When I'm wrong, don't correct me — ask the question that exposes where my
  reasoning broke.
- **Knowledge checkpoint** — teach in five levels (beginner → expert),
  stopping after each to ask one question that proves readiness, and refuse
  to advance until it's answered.
- **Mistake teacher** — given something I got wrong, show where the thinking
  went off, which false assumption caused it, and the one mental model that
  would have prevented it.
- **Retention mode** — after teaching, generate three spaced-repetition
  questions for today, three days out and a week out, plus one real
  situation to apply it in before it fades.

A second saved post made the same case: the value is in the back and forth,
because the model waits, checks the answer, and adjusts the next step.

Directly usable here — retention mode is essentially what this knowledge
base's quiz mode does, and the mistake teacher is a better review pattern
than re-reading an entry.
