From someone recounting five questions they were actually asked.

**"Your favourite product and why?"** Clarify whether they mean software or
physical and pick accordingly. Then reframe it internally as *what product
changed your life* — that raises the stakes and gives the answer a reason to
exist. Lead with the pain it solved and why it's personal.

**"Tell me about leading a team."** Use STORY rather than STAR:

- **S**takes — why does this story matter?
- **T**asks — what did you do?
- **O**pportunity — how did you step up as a leader?
- **R**esult — the impact, with metrics.
- **Y**our learnings — the lesson and how it applies to this role.

**"Walk me through your most technical project."** Don't stay in the code —
go into the technical substance but land on impact, and prepare for
follow-ups.

**"A difficult trade-off you made."** Don't present only the option you
chose. Give the option set, the pros and cons, the decision criteria, and
why those criteria fit. How you decided matters more than what you decided.

**"Design a product for X."** Customize the framework heavily rather than
marching through CIRCLES, and be willing to steer off it. Keep the energy
high and make the interviewer a collaborator rather than a judge.
