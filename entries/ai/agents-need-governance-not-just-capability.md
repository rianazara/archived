Cited from Deloitte: 74% of companies plan to use AI agents by 2027, while
only 21% have a mature way to govern them. The post's argument is that the
gap between those two numbers is the whole story — the constraint on
deployment is trust and oversight, not model ability.

The resulting shape of work: agent does the work, human owns the judgment
and the gate. Which, as the post notes, resembles the job most people
already had.

Connects directly to "permissions before autonomy" in
[[seven-layers-of-ai-engineering]] and to the human-in-the-loop pattern in
[[agent-design-patterns]] — the same insight arriving from the organizational
side rather than the engineering side.

I haven't verified the Deloitte figures against the original report; they're
quoted from the post.
