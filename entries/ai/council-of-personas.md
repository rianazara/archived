The problem it addresses is sycophancy: ask whether your idea is good, your
pricing is right, your plan is sound, and the default answer tends toward
yes. The post's point is that this isn't fixed by a better-worded prompt but
by a different structure.

Instead of one question, stage a debate between roles and then adjudicate:

- **The Contrarian** — looks only for what will fail.
- **The First-Principles Thinker** — rebuilds the question from scratch.
- **The Expansionist** — finds upside you missed.
- **The Outsider** — strips your context and looks at the raw problem.
- **The Executor** — cares only what to do Monday morning.
- **The Chairman** — reads the debate and delivers the verdict.

Why this works where "be critical" doesn't: assigning a role whose entire
job is to find failure makes disagreement the success condition for that
pass, rather than something the model has to weigh against being agreeable.

Same family as [[reflexion-generator-critic-loop]] — adversarial structure
instead of asking nicely for rigor. Worth noting you can run this in one
pass by asking for all five perspectives explicitly; the full setup was
gated behind a comment-for-DM.
