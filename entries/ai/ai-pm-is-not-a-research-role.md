The framing correction: the job isn't becoming a machine learning
researcher, it's turning uncertain capabilities into products people trust
and use.

What to build, in order:

1. **Product fundamentals** — discovery, problem framing, prioritization,
   metrics, experimentation, requirements, tradeoffs. Not optional, not
   replaced by the AI layer.
2. **Technical literacy** — prompts, retrieval, tool use, fine-tuning,
   latency, cost, privacy, failure modes, and *why outputs vary*.
3. **An evaluation mindset** — convert "make it helpful" into measurable
   criteria: define success, enumerate edge cases, name unacceptable
   failures, decide where a human steps in.
4. **Study real workflows** — watch users do the task *before* designing.
   Find the bottleneck, then decide whether AI should assist, recommend,
   generate, or act. Those are four different products.
5. **Portfolio** — an AI product brief, a prototype of one narrow workflow,
   a published evaluation plan with results, and a teardown of an existing
   AI product with concrete improvements.

The closing line is the one to keep: a good AI PM doesn't maximize how much
AI is in the product, they maximize user value with AI as one tool.

Step 4's four verbs are the most useful distinction here — most "AI
features" fail because they chose *act* where users wanted *recommend*.
