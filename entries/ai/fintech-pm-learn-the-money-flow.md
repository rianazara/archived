The questions to be able to answer: where does the money actually go, what
happens when a payment fails, and why does a customer have to verify their
identity?

Ground to cover, and what each explains:

- **Payments basics** — how money moves through the financial system
  (central bank primers are the unglamorous best source).
- **ACH / bank transfers** — the mechanics behind payroll, subscriptions and
  loan repayments.
- **Open banking** — how a user connects their bank account to an app.
- **KYC** — why identity checks sit in onboarding, and why "remove a step to
  improve conversion" isn't always available.
- **Disputes and chargebacks** — what happens when a customer challenges a
  charge.
- **Fraud** — the central product tradeoff: blocking fraud without blocking
  real customers.
- **Ledgers** — how a financial product tracks money between accounts.
- **Webhooks and APIs** — how an app learns a payment succeeded, failed or
  was disputed.

Payment processors and bank-connection providers publish most of this as
free documentation, which is both the best source and a hint about who to
read.

The method is the actually useful part: pick a product you care about, map
its money flow, identify where it can break, and prepare one improvement
you'd make. That converts reading into something concrete to discuss.
