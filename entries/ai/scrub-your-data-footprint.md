An account of someone spending a weekend removing years of personal data
from the internet, using an assistant for the parts that used to need a
lawyer or a developer:

- **Drafting opt-out requests** to data brokers under the applicable
  privacy law, in the right register — CCPA for California, GDPR in the EU,
  and other regional equivalents.
- **Account deletion** — working through a directory of how to delete
  accounts on specific services, since the process differs wildly and is
  often deliberately obscure.
- **Breach triage** — checking which breaches you appear in and ranking them
  by risk, to decide between a password change, enabling 2FA, or deleting
  the account outright.
- **Content that won't come down** — where removal isn't possible, shifting
  to publishing enough that the old material ranks lower.

Why it works: this is high-volume, template-shaped, tedious writing where
the right phrasing matters and the structure repeats. Good delegation.

Two cautions. The claimed totals are one person's self-report. And a
generated legal-sounding letter is still your letter — check that the
statute you're citing actually applies to you before sending, since
residency determines which rights you have.
