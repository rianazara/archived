A 900 sq ft Mumbai apartment whose entire scheme started from the owner's
**wardrobe** — the colours she already instinctively chose. Green emerged
first and then moved through the home in shifting shades of olive, sage and
emerald, warmed with teak veneer and grounded in natural stone.

Why this works better than starting from a reference image: a palette taken
from what someone already gravitates toward will keep feeling right, whereas
one taken from a trend has to be maintained against your own taste. It also
gives a decision rule for every later choice — does this sit in the family
or fight it.

How it shows up across the home: an emerald door with regional detailing,
green marble inlays, a wallpapered cove ceiling, olive herringbone tiles in
the kitchen, with the master bedroom softening to beige so the scheme has
somewhere to rest.

Two things to borrow regardless of colour:

- **One hue in several values** reads as considered; the same hue
  everywhere at one intensity reads as a theme.
- **Give it a release** — a room or two that steps back, or the palette
  becomes relentless.

The brief was described as centred on rootedness rather than reference,
which is the same idea stated as an intention.
