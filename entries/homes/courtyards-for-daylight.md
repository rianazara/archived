A 4,500 sq ft Thrissur residence drawing on Kerala's building tradition:
**two semi-indoor patios act as green courtyards**, pulling daylight deep
into the plan and creating informal places for the family to gather.

The problem they solve is geometric. A large or deep footprint leaves
interior rooms far from any external wall, so no amount of window area on
the perimeter reaches them. A courtyard adds perimeter *inside* the
building — which is why the device recurs independently in hot climates
everywhere, not as decoration but as the only way to light and ventilate a
deep plan without mechanical help.

The secondary effect noted here: the plants organize the space naturally,
so the courtyard does circulation and zoning work as well as daylighting.

Other choices in the project — openness balanced against the continuity
typical of traditional Kerala houses, solid wood surfaces, toxin-free
finishes, Victorian detailing alongside contemporary furniture.

Same strategy as [[turn-the-house-inward]], arrived at from a different
constraint: there for privacy on a bad plot, here for light in a deep one.
