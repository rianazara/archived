Two saved posts make the same case — that the default reach for imported
marble skips materials with centuries of use, lower cost and better
regional fit.

**Flooring**

- **Kadappa stone** (Andhra Pradesh) — dark, dense limestone; takes a
  polish, hides wear.
- **Kota stone** (Rajasthan) — hard-wearing greenish-blue limestone, long
  the default for Indian institutional and domestic floors.
- **Athangudi tiles** (Chettinad) — handmade cement tiles, poured with
  coloured oxides over a patterned glass plate. Vivid geometric patterns,
  made to order.
- **Oxide floors** — pigmented cement, seamless and cool underfoot; common
  in South Indian homes.
- **Terrazzo** — colonial-era chip-in-cement, widespread in Indian
  buildings well before it became fashionable again.
- **Brick flooring** — warm, textured, forgiving.

**Other materials** from the companion post: Makrana marble, lime plaster,
terracotta, bamboo.

Why it's more than aesthetics: each carries different thermal behaviour,
maintenance and craft dependency. Oxide and Athangudi need specific
skilled labour that exists regionally and not elsewhere — which is both the
appeal and the practical constraint.

One post was explicit that this isn't a claim that Indian materials are
better, just that they're routinely overlooked at home.
