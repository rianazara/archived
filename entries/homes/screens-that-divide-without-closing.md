A 1,400 sq ft three-bedroom apartment planned for interconnection rather
than separation. The defining device is a **wooden screen partition** that
separates functions while preserving sightlines — you get definition without
losing the sense of the whole.

Why a screen beats a wall in a small plan: a solid partition subtracts both
floor area and borrowed light, while a slatted one keeps the long views that
make a compact home feel larger, and still signals "this is a different
space."

The rest of the approach follows from the same logic — the living area
anchors social life with generous seating and a swing, dining extends
naturally from it rather than being enclosed, and bedrooms get individual
identity through feature walls and lighting while staying materially
consistent with the whole.

On materials: wood finishes, textured stone, brass detail and layered
lighting in an earthy palette — described as grounded without being heavy,
which is the hard part. Consistency across rooms is what lets a home read as
one place when you can see three rooms at once.
