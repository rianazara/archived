A home can show well and be fairly priced and still carry problems that
only surface after closing. The three categories to check before writing an
offer:

- **The property itself** — condition, systems, what's been altered and
  whether it was permitted.
- **The land it sits on** — boundaries, slope, drainage, access, what can
  and can't be built.
- **What's registered on title** — easements, rights of way, covenants,
  liens, restrictions. These limit what you're allowed to do with the home
  regardless of what you paid.

The point worth keeping: the first category is the one buyers inspect and
the third is the one that constrains them. A registered restriction doesn't
show up in a walkthrough and doesn't care how much you like the view.

The post was a realtor's lead-generation piece and withheld its specific
eight-item list behind a DM, so what's preserved here is the framing. The
practical version is to ask what encumbrances are registered against the
title and get them explained *before* making an offer rather than during
conveyancing — and note that the specifics are jurisdiction-dependent, so
local advice is the actual answer.
