A 900 sq ft Pune apartment, fifteen years old and carrying family
significance, where renovation beat rebuilding — and the method was
questioning every wall and every circulation path rather than treating it as
a cosmetic upgrade.

The concrete result: converting an underused balcony into a bathroom freed
roughly **55 sq ft** of interior, which became a proper dining area and a
compact bar. The footprint didn't change; the allocation did.

The transferable move is auditing by *use* rather than by room label — a
balcony that's only ever a drying rack is floor area doing very little, and
the same question applies to corridors, oversized lobbies and the space
around doors that swing the wrong way.

Supporting choices in the same project: large openings and French doors so
daylight becomes a spatial element, layered lighting, and a restrained
palette (warm oak, limestone-textured walls, cane, soft upholstery) which
keeps a small home from reading as busy.

The underlying claim worth keeping: meaningful change isn't always
rebuilding from scratch, it's reinterpreting what's already there — which
is also cheaper, faster, and keeps whatever the place already means to you.
