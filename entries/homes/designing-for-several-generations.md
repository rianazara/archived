A 4,000 sq ft Mumbai home for grandparents, parents and children, organized
around **transition** rather than division. The stated aim was not to assign
each group a section but to let everyone move freely while still having
somewhere to withdraw to.

How it's structured:

- **A calm shared base** — beige marble floors, warm teak, soft cream tones
  — giving the whole home one steady foundation.
- **Generation-specific expressions opening off it** — a puja room as a
  centre for the elders, a work-from-home corner and a selfie spot for
  newer routines.

The useful principle for any shared household: make the common material
language quiet, and let specificity live in the smaller rooms. The reverse —
strong character everywhere — is what makes multi-generation homes feel like
competing houses stitched together.

The designer's framing was that the home should feel steady but never
static, evolving as the family does. Which argues for flexible rooms with
few built-in commitments, since the work corner and the selfie spot are both
answers to this decade's routines, not the next one's.
