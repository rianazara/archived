A 3,500 sq ft house on an awkward trapezoidal urban plot, designed to turn
away from the street entirely. The exterior is restrained and near-blank,
revealing nothing; the interior unfolds toward a lush internal garden that
every major room faces, with courts, verandas and shaded paths blurring
where architecture stops and landscape starts.

Why this is the right answer to a bad site: if the outlook, noise or
proximity is against you, borrowing light and view from an internal court
gives you both without depending on anything outside your boundary. The
blank street face stops being a sacrifice and becomes the privacy that makes
the inside work.

Two details that carry it: **deep overhangs** (shade and rain protection,
which is what makes semi-outdoor space usable in a tropical climate) and a
**minimal material palette** — here slate stone walls — so the garden is
the thing you notice.

The architects designed it for themselves, and described the discipline as
restraint rather than invention, reinforced by studying architects who
worked with precision and atmosphere instead of excess. Worth noting the
genre convention: house tours describe the intent, rarely the compromises.
