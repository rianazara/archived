Three saved looks, all working the same way: one inherited or traditional
textile is the fixed point, and everything else is designed around it.

- **The anchor.** A bride built her entire ensemble around her mother's
  42-year-old gold tissue and zardozi dupatta — the one thing she knew she
  wanted to carry — with the reasoning that on a day beginning a new family
  it felt right to be wrapped in the one that raised her.
- **The reinvention.** For a cocktail, a Banarasi brocade sari was
  deconstructed into a sculptural corseted silhouette, keeping the original
  drape's character while turning the pallu into the statement. Described as
  honouring tradition without being bound by it.
- **The deliberate wrong colour.** A mehendi ensemble moved away from the
  expected palette to a teal Bandhani Banarasi base with a silk Patola
  appliqué dupatta, layered with mirror work, kasab and cutdana, finished
  with kodi shells and ghungroos that sound as you move.

**Crafts named across them**, useful as vocabulary: zardozi and zari
(metallic thread), Kashida embroidery, bandhani and gharchola (tie-dye
traditions), Patola (double ikat silk), Phulkari (Punjabi floral
embroidery), kasab, cutdana.

The transferable principle: one element you didn't buy for the occasion
gives the rest a reason to cohere — and a palette chosen *against*
expectation reads as a decision rather than a default. Same instinct as
[[palette-from-one-object]].
