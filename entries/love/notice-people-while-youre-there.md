The claim across two saved posts: some relationships change not because the
love goes but because the noticing stops. You get used to the person, stop
sending small messages, stop saying they look good, stop asking about the
things that once mattered. The result is someone who feels **unseen rather
than unloved**, which is a distinction worth holding because the second is
easier to notice and deny than the first.

What's named as the remedy is unglamorous and specific — noticing the tired
smile, the new clothes, the way their face changes when they tell you
something, the small things they still do. And saying it out loud, because
assuming someone knows they're loved is the actual failure mode: "I
noticed," "I'm glad you're here," "I still choose you."

The second post is written as a wife's letter to a husband who never
managed it, and lands on a line worth keeping: that she wasn't asking for
too much, she was asking the wrong person for the right kind of love.

The transferable idea: *love isn't only about staying, it's about paying
attention while you're there.* Attention is the part that requires renewing,
and the part that quietly stops first.
