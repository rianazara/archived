Impressions rather than facts, but a few carry practical signal:

- **Cuba** — striking architecture and famously welcoming people, set
  against potholed roads, crumbling buildings and recurring water and power
  outages. Noted as the place where locals criticised their own government
  most openly.
- **Tajikistan** — described as rough on the stomach: limited variety, poor
  hygiene, most of the group getting ill.
- **Japan** — remarkable for *consistency* rather than peaks. A street
  stall, a neighbourhood izakaya and a convenience-store bento all reliably
  good, which is a different claim from "has great restaurants."
- **Galápagos** — bull sharks, mobula rays in formation, sea lions swimming
  alongside you. Rated the best diving of the lot.
- **China** — the favourite: history, landscapes, enormous cities, and
  people friendlier than expected.

Worth holding loosely. This is one person's experience, infrastructure
complaints date quickly, and "my group got sick" says as much about what
they ate as about a country. The Japan observation is the most useful
because it's about variance rather than ranking — somewhere you can eat
well without research is genuinely different to plan for.
