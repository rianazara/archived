Collected from across the saved hiking posts — the practical layer that
matters more than the trail description.

- **Parking is the real permit.** Popular lots fill by 7:30am on weekends,
  with overflow along the highway. Arrive early or go on a weekday; this
  single factor decides more trips than fitness does.
- **Day-of permits exist, with a queue.** Wilderness permits for overnight
  trips can often be had the morning of at a ranger station — one opening
  at 7am had people arriving at 6am on a weekday.
- **Direction of travel changes the difficulty.** Loops have a gentler
  side; Maple Pass counterclockwise is the example.
- **Crowds concentrate at the trailhead and the first viewpoint.** Walking
  further is the cheapest way to be alone.
- **Download maps offline.** Repeated across several posts, with trails
  described as easy to lose and signal unreliable.
- **Season windows are narrow and crowded.** Larches peak early-to-mid
  October, waterfalls run hardest in spring and early summer — the best
  conditions and the biggest crowds are the same weekend.
- **Check the dog rule per land designation**, not per area: allowed in
  national forest, not in the adjacent national park.
- **Weather-dependent activities shouldn't be scheduled.** Gondolas,
  flights and boat tours want to stay flexible.

Most of these are knowable in advance and invisible in the photo that made
you want to go.
