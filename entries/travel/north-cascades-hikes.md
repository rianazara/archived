One of the least-visited US national parks, with a single road through it
and almost no development — which is the appeal and the reason it takes
planning.

**Hikes, easiest first**

| Trail | Distance / gain | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Thunder Knob | 3.6 mi | easy, family friendly |
| Blue Lake | 4.4 mi / 1,050 ft | short payoff |
| Cascade Pass | 7.4 mi / 1,700 ft | moderate; gateway to Sahale Arm |
| Maple Pass Loop | ~6.4–7.2 mi / ~2,050 ft | larches in autumn |
| Sahale Arm (past Doubtful Lake) | 11.8 mi / 4,000 ft | strenuous |
| Maple Pass → Wing Lake → Black Peak | 15.9 mi / 6,850 ft | experienced hikers only |

**Also worth the drive:** Diablo Lake overlook and the Highway 20 scenic
route, driving over Diablo Dam, the Ross Lake Dam hike, and the town of
Winthrop just outside the park.

**Logistics that actually decide the day**

- **Maple Pass counterclockwise** for a gentler climb.
- **Larches peak early-to-mid October**, which is also when Highway 20
  fills with parked cars. The Rainy Pass lot fills by about 7:30am on
  weekends.
- **Wing Lake thins the crowds** — split off at Heather Pass, cross the
  boulder field to Lewis Lake, then climb steeply.
- **Wilderness permits** are needed for most overnight trails inside the
  park; obtainable day-of at the Marblemount Ranger Station, which opens at
  7am — one account was third in line at 6am on a *Thursday*.
- **Dogs** are allowed in the national forest, not the park.
