The constraint that makes this a real problem: a workplace scent has to be
polished and memorable while staying subtle enough for enclosed,
air-conditioned space where people can't move away from it.

Seven families, each with what it signals:

| Family | Reads as |
|---|---|
| Delicate floral | gentle, broadly inoffensive |
| Citrus | crisp and energising |
| Tea / zen | calm, quietly expensive |
| Clean musk | immaculate, like a pressed shirt |
| Iris and powder | poised, quietly authoritative |
| Bold floral | confident — for presentations |
| Crisp fruity | cheerful, approachable |

Examples given in the original span designer and niche houses — iris and
powder for authority, clean musk for the almost-undetectable option, bold
florals saved for occasions rather than Tuesdays.

The useful part is the mapping of family to impression, which survives any
individual bottle being discontinued. Two practical additions: in shared air
the **projection** matters more than the notes, so apply less than you
would elsewhere; and heat amplifies everything, so a scent tested in winter
can be overwhelming in summer.
