The song was an afterthought — written after the director and A.R. Rahman
saw the rushes of the film's funeral sequence, and conceived not as a
conventional patriotic number but as a mother remembering playing
hide-and-seek with a son who is now gone.

The recording, which is the part worth keeping:

- **Lata Mangeshkar travelled to Chennai** specifically to record in
  Rahman's own studio, having asked to work in his space.
- She **arrived days early and rehearsed for three to four days** before
  recording.
- On the day she **declined the chair** put out for her and **stood at the
  microphone for around eight hours**, working through it with Rahman.
- When a section needed another take, she preferred to **re-sing the whole
  paragraph** rather than patch a single line or word.

That last detail is the craft lesson. Patching a word is faster and
technically invisible, but a phrase sung as a phrase carries continuous
intention — which is why the finished track reads as a conversation between
a mother and her son rather than an assembled vocal.

Song: Luka Chuppi · Rang De Basanti (2006) · composer A.R. Rahman ·
lyrics Prasoon Joshi · sung by Lata Mangeshkar and A.R. Rahman.
