Shreya Ghoshal explaining how singers used to produce delay and echo effects
*vocally* — before the effect was something an engineer added afterwards.

The technique is timing and control: repeating a phrase at a decreasing
volume and a deliberate interval so the voice imitates its own reflection.
It requires holding the pitch steady while dropping dynamics precisely, and
it has to be right in the take, because there's nothing to adjust later.

Why it's worth keeping rather than filing as nostalgia: the constraint
produced a musical decision. A sung echo is phrased — the singer chooses
where it falls and how it decays, as part of the performance. A plugin
applies the same decay everywhere. The effect sounds similar and is doing
something different.

A general pattern across crafts: when a tool makes something trivial, the
version made by hand stops being necessary and becomes a distinguishable
style.
