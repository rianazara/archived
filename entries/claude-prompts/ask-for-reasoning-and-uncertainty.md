Rather than taking an answer at face value, instruct the model up front to
check its own claims, show how it got there, and mark which parts it is
unsure about before committing to a final answer.

The underlying principle: a confident-sounding guess and a well-grounded
answer look identical unless you ask for the seams to show. Requesting
explicit uncertainty converts an unreliable narrator into something you can
audit.

Caveat the post doesn't mention: self-reported confidence is itself a
generated claim, not a measurement. It surfaces where a model *expects* to be
shaky, which correlates with but doesn't equal actual error. For anything
consequential, verify against a source rather than trusting the hedge.

A second saved post made the same point with more detail — that the right
