A four-step method for getting less stilted prose out of an assistant, set
once rather than re-prompted every chat:

1. Point the model at a reference describing the usual tells of AI-generated
   writing.
2. Ask it to enumerate the patterns that make such text obvious — robotic
   phrasing, repetitive sentence shapes, filler transitions, overused em
   dashes.
3. Ask it to convert that list into strict rules, then fold them into a
   single standing instruction.
4. Save that instruction in the persistent settings (custom instructions, or
   project instructions in Claude) so every new conversation inherits it.

The leverage is in step 4: a permanent setting beats remembering to ask.

Worth keeping in perspective — the original post attached its own caveat,
that AI-detector scores are inconsistent and shouldn't be treated as proof,
and that you own whatever goes out under your name. Also note the method
optimizes for *not reading as AI*, which is not the same as writing well.
