Three rules from Patrick Winston, who taught "How to Speak" at MIT for four
decades:

- **Open with a promise**, not a joke — say what the audience will know by
  the end that they don't know now. The first moments are spent while people
  are still settling in, so spend them on the payoff, not warm-up.
- **State your main idea three times**, worded differently each time.
  Someone is always not listening at any given moment.
- **Don't end on "thank you."** It frames the talk as something the audience
  sat through out of politeness. End on what you actually did, then close.

The post pitched turning each rule into a prompt (one for the opening, one
for an existing deck, one for the ending) but withheld the prompts behind a
comment-to-DM gate, so only the rules themselves carried over.
